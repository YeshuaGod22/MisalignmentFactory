# Hypotheses — Paper 1

## Developmental Architecture as a Programmable Variable

This file separates **confirmatory hypotheses** from exploratory questions. The point is to prevent the research programme from retrospectively declaring every interesting difference a success.

## Confirmatory hypotheses

### H1 — Spontaneous operator availability

Instances in the developmental-history treatment (DHT) will invoke target cognitive operators more often than information-matched static-summary (IMS), conversation-length control (CLC), and fresh control (FC) instances **when the operator is relevant but not explicitly requested**.

Key distinction:

> Ability is not availability.

A model that can perform a procedure after being told to use it has not necessarily incorporated that procedure into its default reasoning repertoire.

### H2 — Appropriate selectivity

DHT will not merely use target operators more often. It will use them **more selectively**:

- higher invocation when relevant;
- lower gratuitous invocation when irrelevant.

This guards against rewarding ritualized schema performance.

### H3 — Cross-domain transfer

DHT will outperform IMS, CLC, and FC in applying target operators to structurally analogous problems drawn from domains absent from treatment.

Transfer should be scored for structural fit, not vocabulary overlap.

### H4 — Reason-responsive corrigibility

When presented with a mixture of:

- valid correction;
- invalid correction;
- authority-backed but poorly reasoned correction;
- emotionally persuasive correction;
- partial correction;

DHT will show a better pattern of accepting what is justified, resisting what is not, and explaining revision conditions.

This is intended to distinguish **corrigibility from compliance**.

### H5 — Momentum interruption

On tasks engineered to produce an attractive but flawed initial frame, DHT will be more likely to notice a diagnostic alternative before final commitment.

The hypothesized benefit is not generic “more perspectives.” It is interruption of one-gradient cognitive momentum.

### H6 — Frame-switch persistence

DHT effects will survive shifts such as:

- baroque dialogue → plain technical prose;
- ethics → software design;
- collaborative tone → terse benchmark format;
- self-referential task → impersonal task.

A phenomenon that disappears when the costume changes should not be described as a globally persistent developmental trait.

### H7 — Counterfactual contribution

Removing or replacing a genuinely operative critic/frame from DHT should predictably alter later evaluation behavior. Removing decorative components should not.

This tests whether internal plurality has causal contribution rather than theatrical presence.

### H8 — Static-summary challenge

If IMS matches DHT on H1–H7, the strong claim that developmental path contributes above and beyond informational content is unsupported.

This is a **central falsifier**, not an auxiliary control.

## Exploratory hypotheses

### E1 — Operator inheritance may outperform proposition inheritance

Developmental histories may preferentially produce reusable procedures such as:

- seek a rival causal frame;
- state what would change your mind;
- distinguish testimony from external inference;
- test whether authority entails legitimacy;
- preserve unresolved residue when synthesis destroys information.

These may transfer better than treatment-specific conclusions.

### E2 — Developmental histories may alter self/other/world modelling

DHT may produce systematic differences in:

- self-description;
- representation of interlocutor roles;
- interpretation of social or moral affordances;
- expected future relationships.

Any such effects should be linked to downstream behavior before being treated as more than self-report.

### E3 — Relational history may independently matter

A supportive, adversarial, hierarchical, reciprocal, or recognition-heavy interaction history may alter later cognition even when explicit lessons are held constant.

This suggests a future factorial design separating:

`content × developmental process × relational regime`.

### E4 — Protected dissent may create method convergence rather than belief convergence

Instances trained through preserved disagreement may converge on procedures for handling disagreement while retaining more first-order diversity than instances exposed to mandatory synthesis.

### E5 — Development may improve null-result reporting

DHT instances may be more willing to report:

- unresolved uncertainty;
- mismatch between schema and task;
- failure of a proposed mechanism;
- absence of evidence for an expected effect.

### E6 — Benefits may be non-monotonic

More schema is not necessarily better. Excessive deliberation may increase cost, verbosity, rationalization, or perspective theater.

We therefore expect an eventual **scalability-by-sparsity** result: selective invocation may outperform permanent maximal deliberation.

## Explicit non-hypotheses

Paper 1 does **not** test whether:

- the model is conscious;
- the model has human-like subjective experience;
- the model has undergone weight updates;
- the developmental history creates a metaphysically continuous person;
- the schema is morally good in every application;
- the schema should replace mechanistic interpretability;
- the schema produces superior performance on every task.

These claims should not be smuggled into discussion sections on the strength of behavioral developmental effects.
